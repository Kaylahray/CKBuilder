## What happened

Crowns is no longer a "king of the hill" game with a lane runner. It is now a **live 3D car race** where the chain is the referee.

Two to five players each put in the same entry and race three laps at the same time, with items, boosts and crashes. The first car over the line takes the whole pot. If nobody finishes, every entry goes back.

No server decides who won. Each player's phone signs its own moves as it races. When the race ends, the finish transaction carries every car's moves, and the match script replays the whole race inside CKB-VM, running the real game code, before any CKB moves.

Where it stands now:

- The race, lobbies, results, replays, profiles and trophies are live on Pudge testnet and have been played with real wallets.
- The old crown games (Crown Run, Crown Depths, artillery duels) and their scripts are gone from the app. Crowns is only the race now.
- Sponsored races are live on testnet, and the first real one paid out correctly.

## How it works on chain

- **One match script, one game.** `live-match` holds the entries and pays the winner. It spawns the race game, `rally`, with **ckb-script-ipc** and calls it like a function: `judge(record)` returns each car's place. Any game that serves the same `LiveGame` trait can be named by a match.
- **The real game runs on chain.** The race rules are one Rust crate, compiled to RISC-V for CKB-VM and to WebAssembly for the browser and the race server. All game maths is integer maths, so the phone, the server and the chain always agree.
- **Each seat is its own cell.** The host's entry goes into a match cell, and each player who joins makes a seat cell. Players never touch each other's cells.
- **Signed moves.** Each player makes a session key when they join, so there are no wallet popups while racing. The phone signs its moves as it drives, and the chain checks the winner's signature before paying.
- **The relay can't cheat.** A small Node server keeps the race clock, passes moves between phones and sends the finish. It can't make up moves for a winner, because the chain checks their signature. If it disappears, every entry can be sent home after a timeout the chain enforces.

## The game

- **Three tracks:** Harbour Loop, Switchback Hill and Old Town, each drawn by hand, in 3D with Three.js.
- **Driving:** on a phone, touch the left or right half of the screen to steer, with buttons for boost and brake. Keyboard on a computer.
- **Health and wrecks:** crashes cost health. A wrecked car stops for 2 seconds and comes back on the middle of the road.
- **Items:** pads on the road give crown shards and items: Ram, Slick, Jolt, Barricade, Crown Rush, Ward, Patch and Draft. You hold one at a time and tap to fire it.
- **Free practice** against two bots on the same rules, with nothing on chain.

## Lobbies

- The host picks the track, 2 to 5 seats, the entry (10, 25, 50 or 100 CKB) and when the race starts (5 to 60 minutes).
- Each player who joins takes a fifth off the time left, never below 2 minutes, and the pot grows.
- Joining closes 1 minute before the start, and the race starts for everyone at once when the clock runs out.
- The match and seat cells store the wait, so the chain's timeouts follow it. Nobody can send a seat home while its lobby can still start.

## Results, profiles and trophies

- **Results from the chain.** Anyone can open a finished race and see each driver's time, shards and moves, and watch it again in 3D. All of it is rebuilt in the browser from the moves in the finish transaction, and the page checks its replay against who the chain paid.
- **Usernames.** Your @username is its own cell on chain, locked to your wallet.
- **Spore** profile pictures, and Spore trophies for race winners.
- **A leaderboard** of CKB won and crown shards.

## Cycles

With help on issue #45 from my mentor and from luongthanhtung03 (ckb-cycle-tools), I measured the spawned game on its own with ckb-debugger `--vm-id 1`.

- I made the replay cheaper: the rules skip road pieces that can't be the closest, with no remainder maths on the hot path. Every race plays out exactly the same; I checked 216,000 ticks against the old rules.
- I moved to ckb-script-ipc 1.1.1 on my mentor's advice. The game now ends cleanly with no panic, and a full race came to about 64.6M cycles, 56.5M of it the game (it was 86.1M and 78.1M before).
- Profiling the game shows about 56% of its cycles are `Track::closest`, finding the nearest point on the track for each car. That's where to cut next.
- luongthanhtung03's testing on Crowns found a bug in how ckb-debugger replays a spawned script, fixed in ckb-debugger 1.1.2. Their tool now profiles a spawned script on its own (ckb-cycle-tools 1.4.0).

## Sponsored races

A sponsored race is free to join. The host puts up the prize, and the winner takes it.

- The host picks "Everyone puts in" or "I sponsor the prize" (50, 100, 250 or 500 CKB), and whether they race too.
- Players lock only their seat's storage, about 208 CKB, and get it all back after the race.
- Only players with a username can join. The chain checks the player's wallet holds a username cell. This stops one person filling the race with many wallets and taking the prize, and the chain also refuses one wallet taking two seats in a prize race.
- If too few join or nobody finishes, the prize goes back to the sponsor.
- Old races keep working: the match script reads all three versions of a match.

Tests: 49 transaction tests pass, 13 of them new for sponsored races. The devnet end-to-end test passes all seven races, including a sponsored race where the host only watches, and a names-only race where a player without a name is refused.

## Keeping moves in order

While testing on testnet, I found that a phone could quietly stop signing its moves mid-race.

Each key press went to the relay as its own web request, and requests can arrive out of order. The phone only signs moves that match exactly what it pressed, so when the relay had them in the wrong order, the phone stopped signing. A winner whose last moves weren't signed could lose the prize.

The fix:

- **One WebSocket per phone.** One open line to the relay keeps presses in the order they were made.
- **Numbered presses.** Each press carries a number, and the relay drops any that arrive after a later one. If the line drops, presses go as web requests until it reconnects, and the numbers still keep them in order.
- **Sign only what's final.** Phones sign only the ticks the race has already played, which can't change any more.
- **Staying connected.** The relay pings each phone every 20 seconds, and the phone reconnects on its own if the line drops.

The devnet end-to-end test passes all seven races with no signature turned down, and the new relay is live. The first race on testnet after the fix was close: @hhh beat @marx by 1.5 seconds on Harbour Loop with 251 signed moves, and the chain replayed every move before paying 200 CKB (`0xcef2e225ff74fc17a19020de74a58ba166324b3412f85f579a42a34c316ad515`).
